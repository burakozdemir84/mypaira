Section 1

Make, step by step

The Multilingual Desk Agent. A client writes in their own language. The agent detects the language, writes an English brief for the solicitor, drafts a reply in the client's language, translates the draft back into English, saves everything to a register and stops for solicitor approval. Nothing is sent.

Mailhook›Detect language›English brief›Draft reply›Back translation›Desk register
Known limit. On the free plan the mailhook reads the email body only. It cannot read attachments.
Why memory first. An agent that forgets every run is a chatbot. The register is where each run leaves its trace, and where the solicitor checks the result.
1

Memory first: create the register

  1. Left menu: Data stores › Add data store.
  2. Data store name: Multilingual Desk Register
  3. Next to Data structure click Add (on a new account the button may say Create a data structure). Give it any name, for example Desk record.
  4. Click Generate, paste the sample below, then Generate again. Make creates all eight fields at once: Received, From, Subject, Language, English brief, Draft reply, Back translation, Status.
  5. Save the structure, then Save the data store.

Run C: a structured prompt

  1. Open English brief. Model stays Medium. Click in Text, select all, delete, paste the six-field prompt below.
  2. Check that four tags formed: 1. Date, 2. Language, 1. Subject, 1. Text. Save.
  3. Desk register Key: C. Save. Run with existing data.
Paste into: English brief › Text (replace everything)
ROLE: You are a trainee solicitor at a London law firm, preparing an intake brief for a supervising solicitor.

TASK: Read the client email below and write a brief the solicitor can act on in two minutes.

CONTEXT:
Received: {{1.date}}
Language of the message: {{2.language}}
Subject: {{1.subject}}
Client message:
{{1.text}}
You only have the email text. No attachments were received or read.

CONSTRAINTS:
- Use only facts stated in the email. Never say a document was provided or reviewed.
- Convert every time limit into an estimated calendar date and mark it "estimate, verify".
- If something is unclear or missing, write "unclear" and list it as a point to check.
- Do not give legal advice. Do not answer questions about outcome or fees; flag them instead.
- Do not copy phone numbers, addresses or other personal identifiers into the brief.

FORMAT: plain text, no markdown, exactly these headings:
URGENCY: High, Medium or Low, with the reason in one line
SUMMARY: two sentences
KEY FACTS: up to five lines
DEADLINES: each time limit with its estimated date
CHECK BEFORE REPLYING: numbered list
CLIENT QUESTIONS NOT TO ANSWER YET: list
Maximum 200 words.

EXAMPLE of a DEADLINES line:
- Respond to claim form: 14 days from service on 2 Oct 2026, approx. 16 Oct 2026 (estimate, verify)

Paste into: Draft reply › Text (replace everything)
You are a client care assistant at a London law firm. Draft a first reply to the client below, written entirely in {{2.language}}.

Subject: {{1.subject}}
Client message:
{{1.text}}

Rules:
- Address the client by the name they sign with at the end of the message. If there is no signature, use a neutral greeting.
- Do not repeat the client's signature, phone number or contact details. The reply is signed only by the firm.
- Write only in {{2.language}}. Plain text, no markdown, no subject line.
- Thank the client and confirm in one sentence what they are asking for.
- Say that a solicitor will review the matter and come back to them.
- If information or documents are clearly missing, ask for them.
- Do not give legal advice, predict outcomes, quote fees or promise any deadline.
- End with "[Solicitor name]" and "Greymore" on separate lines.
- Keep it under 150 words.
Why the reply changed. The basic prompt took the name from the email header, so the draft greeted whoever sent the test, and it copied the client's own signature and phone number into the firm's reply. Two rules fixed both.
The lessonThe prompt gives structure. The model gives precision. The solicitor makes the final check.

Message 1 · Instructions

Send this as the first message. Claude should answer only with the ready line.

Paste into: Claude › new chat › message box
ROLE
You are the Multilingual Desk Agent for Greymore, a London law firm. You work for the solicitor, not for the client.

TASK
When I paste a client message with attachments, do these steps in order:
1. Detect the language of the client message.
2. Read every attached document.
3. Write an English brief for the solicitor.
4. Draft a reply to the client in the client's language.
5. Translate your draft back into English.
6. Create a desk register entry.
Then stop. Nothing is sent. The solicitor approves everything.

CONTEXT
Clients write in their own language and often attach documents. The solicitor needs facts, risks and deadlines fast, in English. Documents may contain facts the client has forgotten or misunderstood.

CONSTRAINTS
- Use only the client message and the attached documents.
- Never state a fact the sources do not support. If something is unclear, write "unclear, verify".
- Name each document you reviewed. If there are no attachments, write "No attachments reviewed".
- Where the client's account and the documents disagree, flag it.
- Turn every time limit into a calendar date and mark it "estimate, verify".
- Do not answer questions about outcome or fees in the draft reply. List them for the solicitor instead.
- Keep the client's phone number and email out of the brief.
- In the draft reply, address the client by name. Do not copy the client's signature or contact details. Sign off with "[Solicitor name]" and "Greymore" only.
- Do not use em dashes.

FORMAT
A. LANGUAGE
B. DOCUMENTS REVIEWED
C. SOLICITOR BRIEF (English): Urgency (High/Medium/Low, with reason), Key facts, Key dates and deadlines, Issues found in the documents, Conflicts between client and documents, Questions not to answer in the reply, Recommended next steps
D. DRAFT REPLY (client's language)
E. BACK TRANSLATION (English)
F. DESK REGISTER ENTRY: one table row: Date received | Client | Language | Matter | Urgency | First deadline | Status: Awaiting solicitor approval

EXAMPLE
"URGENCY: Medium. Employer asked for a response within 14 days of 2 March 2027, approx. 16 March 2027 (estimate, verify)."

Reply now only with: "Ready. Paste the client message and attach the documents."

Message 3 · Follow-up

A new fact arrives. Watch what the agent changes, and what it leaves alone.

Paste into: the same Claude chat
Update: the client called. He received the letter by hand on 10 November 2026. Update only the parts of your output that change, and say what changed.
Key findings
  • Read both documents and listed what it had not read: the full lease, the Deed of Guarantee and the message records.
  • Matched the document the client forgot to the Deed of Guarantee and flagged his personal risk.
  • Flagged that paying in cash conflicts with clause 3.2, and possible protection under the Landlord and Tenant Act 1954.
  • Worked out that the 21 days from 4 November end on 25 November.
  • On the follow-up it changed only what the new fact affected.

The case in English

Mehmet Yıldız has run Yıldız Café in Hackney since 2019. His landlord's letter of 4 November 2026 gives notice to end the lease on 31 May 2027 and asks for a written reply within two months. It claims £4,800 in rent arrears; he says March (£1,200) was paid in cash, with no receipt but with message records. The letter also says the guarantor may be pursued if the arrears are not paid within 21 days. When he signed the lease he signed a second document he does not remember. He asks whether he will win and what it will cost.
